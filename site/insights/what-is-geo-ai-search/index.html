What Is GEO? A Guide to Ranking in AI Search

Posted by

MYSense Team

Digital Marketing Agency Malaysia

Our blog

  • October 5, 2026

A foundational guide to generative engine optimisation, what it is, how it differs from SEO, how AI systems select citations, and how to get started.

Key Takeaways

GEO stands for generative engine optimisation, the practice of structuring a website so AI systems like ChatGPT and Google AI Overviews cite it in their answers. ChatGPT alone holds roughly 75% of AI chatbot use in Malaysia as of June 2026, and most ai seo services on the market today are really GEO work under a broader name.

  • GEO targets citation inside an AI answer, not a ranked link position
  • Most GEO signals are extensions of solid SEO, not a separate discipline
  • AI systems retrieve and cite sources differently to how Google ranks them
  • GEO cannot be guaranteed, no agency controls how an AI selects citations
  • Start with technical readiness before investing in specialist GEO work

GEO, generative engine optimisation, is the term for making sure AI systems cite your business when they answer a question your customers might ask. It is newer than SEO, less standardised, and surrounded by more marketing hype than substance. This guide covers what GEO actually is, how it differs from traditional SEO, and how to start without falling for guarantee-style claims that do not hold up.

Google’s own position, published in its generative AI optimisation guide, is that optimising for AI search is still fundamentally SEO, grounded in the same crawlable, indexed, trustworthy content the core search index has always rewarded. Checking that guidance directly against Google’s official documentation is a reasonable starting point before paying for anything marketed as a separate GEO product.

What Does GEO Actually Stand For?

Generative engine optimisation, the practice of structuring a brand’s content and technical presence so AI systems like ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini and Google AI Overviews cite it when generating an answer. Unlike SEO, which earns a ranked position in a list of links, GEO earns a mention or citation inside a synthesised, conversational response, sometimes without the user ever clicking through to a website at all.

How Is GEO Different From Traditional SEO?

The underlying goal, being found and trusted, is the same, but what each discipline optimises for and how it is measured genuinely differs. The table below sets out the key differences.

How Does an AI System Actually Decide What to Cite?

Most AI answer engines use a retrieval step, pulling candidate source passages related to the question, before a language model synthesises those sources into one answer and names the ones it drew from. A page has to be crawlable and indexed to be retrieved at all, then clearly structured enough that the model can confidently extract and attribute a fact to it, which is why front-loaded, direct answers and clean sourcing matter more here than clever phrasing.

Does GEO Replace SEO, or Work Alongside It?

Alongside it, not instead of it. AI Overviews and AI Mode are grounded in Google’s core search index, so a page that cannot be crawled and trusted by classic search cannot be cited by the AI layer sitting on top of it. Most of what makes a page GEO-ready, technical health, clear sourcing, front-loaded answers, is the same work that earns strong traditional SEO rankings.

How Do You Get Started With GEO?

Start by confirming the technical basics, your key pages are indexed, load quickly, and present their facts as plain, selectable text rather than locked inside images or scripts. From there, add a named author and real sourcing to your most important pages, and structure the top of each page as a direct, self-contained answer before any narrative build-up. Only after those fundamentals are solid does specialist entity and citation work start adding meaningful further value.

Frequently Asked Questions About GEO

1. Is GEO the same as AEO?

Closely related but not identical. AEO, answer engine optimisation, generally refers to winning featured snippets and direct answer boxes within classic search, while GEO specifically targets citation inside generative AI responses. The terms are often used loosely and interchangeably in practice.

2. How long does GEO take to show results?

Similar to SEO, expect initial signal changes within four to six weeks and more meaningful citation improvements over three to six months. There is no fast, reliable shortcut, be sceptical of any provider promising rapid guaranteed results

3. Can a small business do basic GEO without an agency?

Yes, for the fundamentals. Ensuring content is readable as plain text, front-loading answers, and adding a named author with real sourcing are all things a business can implement directly without specialist help.

4. Does GEO cost extra on top of an existing SEO retainer?

It depends on the provider. Since most GEO work overlaps heavily with strong SEO practice, a good provider should already be incorporating much of it, ask directly what a separate GEO line item would add beyond what solid SEO already covers.

5. Is there a way to check if my content is being cited by AI tools?

Run a consistent set of relevant prompts across ChatGPT, Google AI Mode and Perplexity periodically and record whether your business appears. There is no single unified dashboard across platforms yet, so this currently requires manual, repeated checking.

GEO Is an Extension of Good SEO, Not a Separate Game

The businesses getting genuine value from GEO right now are mostly the ones with strong SEO fundamentals already in place, adding citation-specific refinements on top. Google’s core ranking systems are global. The same signals, including relevance, authority, page experience and technical health, apply to every site. What differs in Malaysia is the competitive environment for specific keywords and the local intent signals Google reads from searches made in Malaysia. A site targeting Malaysian commercial queries benefits from localised content, a Malaysian IP address and locally relevant backlinks, but the underlying ranking systems are identical to those applied elsewhere.

Google selects what it considers the most relevant and authoritative page for each query, based on the signals across all indexed pages on the site. If multiple pages target the same keyword without a clear hierarchy, Google may split authority between them and rank none well. An SEO expert in Malaysia should map each target keyword to a single designated page and ensure all other pages either support that page through internal links or target different terms.

Publishing content improves rankings only if the content addresses genuine search queries that the site is not already covering, is well-structured and internally linked to the relevant service pages, and is published on a site with clean technical foundations. Publishing content on a technically broken site, or targeting keywords the site has no authority to rank for, produces very little measurable impact. Volume without strategy is not a substitute for audit-led SEO services.

PageRank is one of Google’s original core ranking systems, which uses the structure of links between pages to assess which pages are most authoritative for a given topic. Google has confirmed it continues to use PageRank as part of its core systems. In practical terms, this means that links from credible, relevant third-party sites remain one of the most significant controllable signals available to an SEO programme. Agencies that do not include link acquisition in their SEO services are addressing only part of the ranking system.

Google makes thousands of updates to its ranking systems each year, most of them small and unannounced. It also publishes confirmed core updates several times a year that can produce significant ranking shifts across many sites simultaneously. Maintaining a stable ranking requires ongoing monitoring through Google Search Console, regular technical health checks and a programme that is built on durable signals rather than tactics that exploit short-term loopholes.

A ranking factor is a signal Google uses as an input: page speed, backlinks, keyword relevance. A ranking system is the automated process that evaluates those factors and produces a ranked result. Google has many ranking systems, including RankBrain, BERT, neural matching and PageRank, each assessing different aspects of a page. Effective Google search engine optimisation in Malaysia addresses the inputs to multiple systems rather than optimising for any single factor in isolation.
